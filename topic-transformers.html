Transformers & Attention

The architecture that changed AI - Self-Attention, Multi-head Attention, and more

📌 Quick Summary

🧠
Self-Attention

Allows tokens to "look" at other tokens in the sequence to capture context, regardless of distance.

🎭
Multi-Head Attention

Runs multiple attention mechanisms in parallel to focus on different aspects of the input (e.g., syntax vs semantics).

🏗️
Encoder-Decoder

Original architecture for translation. BERT (Encoder-only), GPT (Decoder-only), T5 (Both).

📖 The Scaled Dot-Product Attention

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ)V

Where:

  • Q (Query): What I'm looking for.
  • K (Key): What I have to offer.
  • V (Value): The actual information.
  • √dₖ: Scaling factor to prevent large dot-products from pushing softmax into small gradient regions.

Why Transformers?

Unlike RNNs, Transformers process entire sequences at once (parallelization). They solve the vanishing gradient problem in long sequences through direct token-to-token connections.

🎤 Interview Q&A

Q1 What is the purpose of Positional Encodings? +

Answer:

Since Transformers have no recurrence or convolution, they are permutation-invariant (they don't know the order of words). Positional encodings are added to input embeddings to inject information about the position of tokens in the sequence.

Q2 BERT vs GPT - what is the fundamental difference? +

Answer:

  • BERT: Encoder-only. Uses bidirectional attention (can see future and past tokens). Best for understanding tasks like classification or NER.
  • GPT: Decoder-only. Uses masked self-attention (can only see past tokens). Best for generative tasks.

✍️ Practice Problem

Why is the dot product QKᵀ divided by √dₖ?

Divide by √dₖ to scale down the dot product. For large values of dₖ, the dot products grow large in magnitude, pushing the softmax function into regions where it has extremely small gradients, leading to vanishing gradients during backpropagation.
